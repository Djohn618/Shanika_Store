Since 2014

From a coconut-leaf stall to a Kandy Road landmark.

Shanika Super & Grocery is named after the woman who helped build it. This is the story of a family, a five-perch plot of land and more than ten years of hard work.

  1. 2014

    How it all started

    In 2014, Bandu Kumara John Kothalawalage bought a small five-perch plot of land near the roundabout in Trincomalee, No. 119. With his wife and a few family members, he built a humble vegetable shop out of wood and coconut leaves and named it Shanika Super, after his wife.

    There was no electricity and no running water. A car battery powered three LED bulbs. It took many sleepless nights and a lot of determination to keep the little shop going.

  2. 2014 – 2019

    Growing, one market trip at a time

    Every two months, Bandu Kumara travelled to Dambulla, famous for its vegetable and fruit markets. Little by little the shop added rice, beans, sugar and other groceries. As word spread among locals, earnings grew, and the shop finally got electricity and water.

    Determined to make it permanent, he took a loan from People's Bank, right across the road, and rebuilt the wooden stall as a proper shop. Shanika Super & Grocery was born.

  3. 2015 – 2019

    Giving back to the community

    As the business grew, so did its generosity. The family helped people in need with food, school supplies and healthcare, and supported Buddhist, Hindu, Christian and Muslim communities alike.

    Success brought rivals as well as friends, but perseverance and kindness kept the shop growing.

  4. 2019 – today

    A new chapter

    In 2019, Bandu Kumara moved to Switzerland with his family and handed the business to his wife's brother, Nipuna Sadaruwan Alenthuge. Today the shop serves local families and also supplies companies and government institutions.

    Under Nipuna's care, Shanika Super & Grocery is busier, better stocked and in better shape than ever.
